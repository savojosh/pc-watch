## Reading the market

The earlier $115 estimate was **pessimistic by ~$40**. Fully modular 750 W Gold ATX 3.1
units are readily available in the low $70s. This is a competitive, commoditized category.

**The dream build's $214.90 Titanium 1000 W unit was over-specified by ~$135** for a system
that peaks around 550 W. The headroom argument for it is real but does not survive the price
comparison: if a 2029 GPU genuinely needs more than 750 W, buying a second PSU then still
costs less than the premium today.

## Buy triggers

- **750 W Gold, ATX 3.1, fully modular at ≤ $80**: buy.
- **At ≤ $70**: strong buy.
- **850 W Gold at ≤ $90**: buy — a small step up in headroom for little money is fine.
- Do not pay above $100 for a 750 W unit in this build.

## Hard rule — do not track used

**Bare PSU listings on Marketplace should be ignored outright, at any price, from any
seller.** This is the one part where the no-heavily-used rule is absolute rather than a
preference:

- Degradation is completely invisible from a listing or a photo
- Failure modes can damage every component downstream
- The savings are trivial — $30 on a $75 part
- The warranty is most of what you are buying

A PSU included inside a whole-system purchase is a different matter and is judged on the
system's merits.

## Do not track

Used or open-box PSUs. Bronze-rated units. Non-modular units. SFX units (wrong form factor
for an ATX mid tower). Anything below 750 W — the savings are trivial and the headroom is
the entire point of the spec.
