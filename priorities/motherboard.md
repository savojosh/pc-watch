# Priorities — Motherboard

## What I actually want from this part

To never think about it again for five years. The board is not where I want performance;
it is where I want optionality. It should quietly accept every upgrade I make until 2031
and not be the reason I have to rebuild.

## Ranked priorities

1. **Socket longevity.** This is the whole ballgame. AM5 through 2029 with Zen 6 confirmed
   is why this platform was chosen over the dream build's LGA1851.
2. **4 DIMM slots.** Non-negotiable — the RAM plan is staged (32 → 64 → 128 GB) and a
   2-slot board would force throwing kits away.
3. **Expansion headroom that survives being fully populated.** Deciding factor in the pick.
4. **VRM strong enough for a chip I haven't bought yet** — specifically a Zen 6 X3D in 2027–28.
5. Networking. Wanted 2.5GbE+; got 5GbE with a Linux asterisk.

## Realistic expectations

- **A board is not a performance part.** Spending more than ~$230 here buys features, not
  speed. The $170 ASRock would have been fine; the $60 premium buys the guaranteed-usable
  expansion slot specifically.
- **The Realtek NIC will probably annoy me on Linux.** Plan on ~$30 for an Intel card rather
  than being surprised. That is a known cost, not a defect.
- **Zen 6 will need a BIOS update** and possibly a flashback with no CPU installed. Normal.
- USB4 is absent. I don't currently have a use for it and shouldn't pay for one.

## What I'd trade away

RGB, fancy heatsinks, onboard audio quality, and 10GbE. None of them matter for this
machine.

## What I would not trade

DIMM slot count and the usable x4 slot. Those are the two things that make this board a
five-year board instead of a two-year one.

## Future goal

When the Zen 6 X3D goes in around 2027–28, this board should accept it with a BIOS update
and nothing else. The displaced CPU and its board then become the always-on trading box —
so this board's real end-of-life is well past 2031.
