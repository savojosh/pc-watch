# Priorities — Case

## What I actually want from this part

Airflow and radiator clearance. Nothing else. This is a purely functional purchase and I
should treat any spending above ~$100 as a mistake.

## Ranked priorities

1. **360 mm radiator clearance**, verified for the specific mounting position.
2. **Genuine airflow** — mesh front, not glass. The overnight thermal load is the reason.
3. **ATX support** and ~330 mm of GPU clearance.
4. **Enough included fans** that I'm not immediately buying more.
5. Looks. The machine will mostly be running unattended.

## Realistic expectations

- **This is a stable, healthy market with no shortage pressure.** $85–100 buys a good case
  and there is no reason to rush or to stretch. Buy on any ordinary sale.
- **Radiator clearance is where builds go wrong.** "Supports 360 mm" in a spec sheet
  sometimes means front-only, or top-only-with-thin-fans, or top-only-if-the-RAM-is-short.
  Verify the exact position I plan to use before buying.
- **A case bundle is not a cooling solution.** The $100 Lancool 205 + CM Elite 240 listing is
  good value *as a case*; the bundled 240 mm AIO doesn't meet the cooling spec.
- **Glass fronts cost real degrees.** The Corsair 680X has excellent radiator support and is
  genuinely tempting, but it cuts against the airflow priority.

## What changed

**Drive bays are no longer a requirement.** The NAS is now a separate box, built later, so
this build is all-NVMe. That decision frees the case choice considerably — the 3.5" bay
count that would have ruled out several good cases is now irrelevant.

## What I'd trade away

RGB, tempered glass, fan controllers, vertical GPU mounts, cable-management theatre, brand.

## What I would not trade

360 mm clearance or front-panel airflow. Those are the two things I'm actually buying.

## Future goal

Never touch this part again. A good airflow ATX case has no upgrade path and needs none —
it should carry the Zen 6 upgrade, the GPU upgrade, and outlive the five-year window
entirely.
